1. Core Concept & Game Loop: "Codeless CP"To turn competitive programming into a mobile-friendly game without writing syntax, Koda should focus entirely on algorithmic intuition and problem modeling—the exact phase of solving a problem that happens before touching a keyboard.The Core Loop (Per Problem)The Encounter (Reading Phase): You are presented with a clean, mobile-formatted problem statement, constraints ($N, M$, time/memory limits), and sample cases. A subtle timer runs in the background (not to punish you, but to track your "time-to-insight").The Hypothesis (Structured Deduction): Instead of an open text box—which is tedious to type on mobile—you build your solution using structured chips/cards across four dimensions:Paradigm / Idea: Dynamic Programming, Greedy, Divide & Conquer, Ad-Hoc, Brute Force, Two Pointers, Binary Search on Answer, etc.Core Algorithm: Dijkstra, BFS/DFS, Segment Tree, DSU, KMP, Sieve, Topological Sort, etc.Data Structures: Priority Queue, Hash Map, Monotonic Stack, Fenwick Tree, Sparse Table, etc.Key Twist / Adaptation (Crucial Step): Every good CP problem has a catch. You select or match the specific modification needed (e.g., "Reverse the graph edges", "Compress coordinates first", "State is (index, mask)", "Sort by end time instead of start time").The Blueprint (Optional Pseudocode / Scratchpad): A lightweight, distraction-free notepad (with quick-insert math/code symbols like →, ∞, [], for, dp[i]) where you jot down the state transition, recurrence relation, or edge cases to implement later when you are at a computer.The Reveal & Scoring: You submit your hypothesis and compare it against the problem's Editorial Solution(s). Since problems often have multiple valid approaches (e.g., Binary Search + Greedy vs. DP), Koda checks if your combination matches any valid solution path registered for that problem.
2. Gamification Mechanics (Making It Feel Like a Game)To keep daily sessions engaging, treat problem-solving like a tactical RPG or puzzle game:Constraint Radar (Mini-Game / Bonus Points): Before answering, Koda highlights the constraints (e.g., $N \le 2 \times 10^5$, $T \le 2\text{s}$). Guessing the target time complexity (e.g., $O(N \log N)$) awards bonus XP and trains your instinct for ruling out TLE approaches immediately.Streak & "Daily Set" (3-Problem Run): Instead of endless scrolling, offer a daily run of 3 problems: 1 Warm-up (comfort zone), 1 Target (current rating/difficulty), and 1 Boss (above your current level or a weak topic).Spaced Repetition (SRS) for Missed Twists: If you fail to identify the right paradigm or twist on a problem, Koda puts it into a "Review Deck" and resurfaces it days or weeks later—preventing you from memorizing answers while ensuring you master the underlying pattern.Implement Queue ("To-Code Backlog"): Any problem where you wrote pseudocode or want to practice coding gets flagged into a "Ready to Implement" queue. Later, when you sit at your PC, you open Koda Web/Sync, grab your saved pseudocode, and submit it to the actual online judge (Codeforces, CSES, AtCoder, etc.).
3. Data Architecture & Problem DatabaseSince you want a large, non-repetitive database with support for multiple valid solutions, your schema should separate the Problem from its Solution Approaches: 
Problems
 ├── id, title, source (CF, CSES, AtCoder, USACO), url, difficulty_rating
 ├── statement_md, constraints_text, sample_io
 ├── target_complexity (e.g., "O(N log N)")
 └── is_solved, attempt_count, next_review_date

Solution_Approaches (1-to-Many with Problems)
 ├── id, problem_id, approach_name (e.g., "Standard DP" vs "Greedy + Heap")
 ├── paradigms: ["Dynamic Programming"]
 ├── algorithms: ["Knapsack"]
 ├── data_structures: ["1D Array"]
 ├── key_insights: ["Iterate weights backwards to save space", "Sort items by ratio"]
 └── editorial_summary_md

User_Attempts
 ├── id, problem_id, timestamp, time_spent_sec
 ├── selected_paradigms, selected_algorithms, selected_ds
 ├── user_twist_notes, pseudocode_draft
 ├── score_accuracy (0–100%), matched_approach_id
 └── status ("Drafted" | "Implemented & AC" | "Needs Review")
Populating the Database Without BurnoutManually typing hundreds of problems will kill your momentum. Since you are using Cursor:Write a small ingestion script (or use an LLM pipeline) that takes raw problem URLs/texts (like CSES or Codeforces lists) and automatically extracts the statement, formats the LaTeX, tags the paradigms/algorithms, and generates the Solution_Approaches JSON—which you then review and import into Koda.

Performance & Analytics DashboardKeep the metrics actionable so they directly guide what you study next:Topic Mastery Radar / Heatmap: Tracks accuracy and solve speed across major categories (Graphs, DP, Greedy, Math/Number Theory, Trees, Strings, Data Structures). Clearly highlights Blind Spots (e.g., "You spot Graph problems 90% of the time, but your DP state-transition accuracy is 45%").Pipeline Funnel: Tracks the full lifecycle of your training:Diagnosed (Solved conceptually in Koda)Blueprinted (Pseudocode written)Implemented (Actually coded and got AC on the online judge)Mistake Breakdown: Categorizes why you missed a problem: Wrong Paradigm, Missed Constraint/TLE, or Missed Edge Case/Twist.5. Recommended Tech Stack (Optimized for Cursor + Mobile)Frontend: React Native (Expo) or React + Vite + Capacitor (gives you a native iOS/Android app from a single TypeScript codebase, with full offline support for reading and drafting anywhere).Local-First Storage + Sync: SQLite (via Expo SQLite) or Supabase (PostgreSQL) with local caching so the app works instantaneously on the go without waiting on network requests, while syncing your pseudocode drafts to your desktop.UI/UX Details for Mobile:Dark mode by default (#121212 / #191919) with crisp KaTeX / MathJax rendering for math formulas ($O(N^2)$, $\sum a_i$).Tag-chip selection with fuzzy search so picking Segment Tree or Binary Search takes two taps instead of typing.
